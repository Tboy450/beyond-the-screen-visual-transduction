# 13. Creative Engineering Concepts

This chapter expands the possibility space. The goal is to move ideas from
vague science-fiction claims toward explicit conceptual architectures with
targets, carriers, transducers, workarounds, and limits.

Speculative ideas are not second-class entries here. They are where many of the
best questions live. The only requirement is that the idea should become more
specific as it is explored: name the target, name the carrier, name the missing
transducer, and name the kind of evidence that would make the concept less
imaginary.

## The Main Engineering Trick

The naive goal is:

```text
send full-resolution images directly into the brain
```

The more plausible engineering direction is:

```text
reduce the information
-> choose a safer carrier
-> use a real transducer
-> calibrate the person's perceptual map
-> train a stable artificial code
-> preserve consent and control
```

## Removing, Rerouting, Or Retaining The Middle Medium

Useful concepts examine every handoff rather than assuming that every layer
must remain or must disappear. A display surface is useful when it improves
safety, precision, learning, or control. A different middle layer may be the
right answer when it adds a needed transduction, feedback path, or form of user
agency.

Examples:

- Replace full video with edge, motion, depth, and semantic cues.
- Replace a fixed screen with active retinal projection or wearable cueing.
- Replace universal pixel grids with personal percept maps.
- Replace passive viewing with active scanning and user questions.
- Replace direct memory-writing claims with voluntary cueing and recall tools.

The design target becomes:

```text
minimal reliable cue
-> active human interpretation
-> useful action
```

[Project Tracks: Perception As The Medium](../PROJECT_TRACKS.md).

## Less-To-More Conceptual Route

| Level | Concept | Why It Is More Concrete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Near-eye display | Uses normal visual biology. |
| 2 | Retinal projection | Removes wall screen but still uses photoreceptors. |
| 3 | Event-based cue display | Reduces bandwidth by sending changes. |
| 4 | Foveated assistive display | Uses gaze and attention to allocate detail. |
| 5 | Retinal prosthesis | Enters the retina with electrodes or light-sensitive cells. |
| 6 | Cortical shape tracing | Uses dynamic stimulation rather than isolated dots. |
| 7 | Learned phosphene language | Stops pretending phosphenes are pixels. |
| 8 | Semantic scene interface | Sends useful meanings instead of images. |
| 9 | Hybrid multisensory interface | Splits information across sight, sound, and touch. |
| 10 | Memory cue system | Supports recall without claiming direct memory writing. |

## Concept Cards

### Artificial Fovea

- Status: Prototype/speculative
- Target: Near-eye display, retinal projector, or prosthetic interface
- Workaround: Use high detail only where the user is looking or attending.
- Why it helps: Real vision already has high acuity in the fovea and lower
  detail in the periphery.
- Limit: Needs gaze tracking and fast recalibration.

### Event-Based Visual Stream

- Status: Prototype
- Target: Assistive display or neural interface front end
- Workaround: Transmit changes, motion, and edges instead of full frames.
- Why it helps: Reduces bandwidth and emphasizes behaviorally useful events.
- Limit: Static objects can disappear unless the system adds refresh or object
  memory.

### Phosphene Font

- Status: Research/speculative
- Target: Retinal or cortical prosthesis
- Workaround: Use a small library of reliable shapes, flashes, arrows, and
  symbols.
- Why it helps: A stable alphabet may be more useful than a noisy low-res image.
- Limit: Requires training and may not feel like natural sight.

### Scene Skeleton

- Status: Speculative/prototype
- Target: Wearable assistive system, semantic overlay, or future prosthesis
- Workaround: Encode the environment as a graph of paths, walls, obstacles,
  people, stairs, doors, and moving objects.
- Why it helps: Navigation often needs structure more than texture.
- Limit: Computer vision errors could mislead the user.

### Depth-First Vision

- Status: Prototype
- Target: Mobility assistance or prosthetic preprocessing
- Workaround: Prioritize distance, collision risk, step edges, and motion.
- Why it helps: Depth cues can be more useful than color for navigation.
- Limit: Not enough for reading, faces, or detailed object recognition.

### Retinal Interpreter

- Status: Speculative
- Target: Retinal implants or optogenetic interfaces
- Workaround: Transform camera data into retina-like contrast, timing, and
  motion signals before stimulation.
- Why it helps: The retina expects processed visual features, not raw pixels.
- Limit: Different retinal diseases leave different circuits intact.

### Cortical Handwriting

- Status: Research direction
- Target: Visual cortex
- Workaround: Trace lines or letters over time rather than activating static
  spots all at once.
- Why it helps: Motion and temporal continuity may help the brain bind dots into
  forms.
- Limit: Requires stable phosphene placement and invasive mapping.

### Cross-Modal HUD

- Status: Prototype
- Target: Hearing, touch, residual vision, or a combination
- Workaround: Split the visual scene into multiple channels.
- Why it helps: A weak visual channel can be supported by sound or touch.
- Limit: Too many cues can overload attention.

### RF-Powered Local Transducer

- Status: Speculative
- Target: Implanted or wearable converter
- Workaround: RF is only communication or power; local hardware performs the
  actual transduction.
- Why it helps: It replaces vague remote-field claims with a concrete receiver.
- Limit: Implant safety, heat, power, and biological targeting remain hard.

### Consent-Locked Interface

- Status: Design rule
- Target: Any cognitive or neural system
- Workaround: Require active, continuous, user-visible authorization.
- Why it helps: It makes autonomy a technical requirement, not an afterthought.
- Limit: The interface must be designed so consent cannot be bypassed silently.

## Workaround Patterns

| Problem | Workaround |
| --- | --- |
| Too little resolution | Use symbols, edges, motion, and semantic labels. |
| Weak spatial control | Calibrate the personal percept map. |
| Low bandwidth | Use event-based, foveated, or task-aware encoding. |
| Noisy percepts | Use redundant cues across multiple senses. |
| Slow learning | Keep cue vocabulary small and consistent. |
| Device drift | Use closed-loop recalibration. |
| Unsafe carrier | Move the carrier outside the body or add a local transducer. |
| Memory-writing fantasy | Convert the idea into voluntary cueing and recall support. |
