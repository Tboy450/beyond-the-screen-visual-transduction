# The Screenless Sight Codex

### An Encyclopedia of Visual Transduction, from Signals to Perception

**Tristan Hash**

A picture on a screen is one way to deliver visual information. This book
examines the larger chain: how information is encoded, carried through a
physical medium, converted into a stimulus, and interpreted by a person.

Its subjects range from CRTs, LCDs, and retinal optics to visual prostheses,
sensory substitution, learned cue systems, and the frontier between perception
and memory. Established explanations sit alongside research summaries and
clearly labeled proposals, so readers can explore both what works and what
would need to be discovered.

## The Central Question

> How can useful visual information reach a person when we change the screen,
> the carrier, the biological interface, or the form of the message itself?

The book returns to one organizing chain:

```text
information -> encoding -> carrier -> transducer -> sensory interface
            -> neural processing -> perception -> interpretation or action
```

A **carrier** transports a signal. A **transducer** converts a physical input
into another form. An **encoding** determines how information is represented.
Separating these roles helps reveal both the possibilities and the missing
pieces in a proposed technology.

## Reading the Evidence

| Label | What it means here |
| --- | --- |
| Established | A well-supported physical, biological, or engineering principle within the stated scope. |
| Research | A cited investigation or reported result; its population, task, and limitations matter. |
| Proposed | A design concept for which this book does not document a working implementation. |
| Speculative | An architecture whose essential mechanism or integration remains unresolved. |
| Frontier | An exploratory idea with major gaps between its premise and demonstrated capability. |

These labels describe claims, not a ranking of imagination or usefulness.
A demonstrated component does not establish the whole system built around it.
The [evidence guide](sources/evidence-guide.md) gives examples and explains
how proposals become stronger entries. Earlier drafts used “Prototype” for
some concepts; these are now labeled “Proposed” where no implementation is
documented.

## Scope and Sources

This is an encyclopedia-style manuscript maintained as linked text. It is
intended for reading, comparison, and conceptual exploration. Its clinical
chapters describe research at an explanatory level; its
[ethics chapter](modules/11-safety-and-ethics.md) sets the boundaries for
interventions involving the eye, nervous system, or memory.

The [bibliography](sources/bibliography.md) and
[annotated research notes](sources/annotated-research-notes.md) provide the
source trail. The [original ChatGPT conversation](https://chatgpt.com/share/6a94fe30-7e04-83ea-84de-37170c4fafe7)
is retained as background to the book's development. Scientific claims should
be checked against the publications themselves.
