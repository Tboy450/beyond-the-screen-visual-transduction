## The Screenless Sight Codex Publishes An Open Map Of Visual Transduction Technology

New documentation project organizes display systems, signal carriers,
transducers, prosthetic vision, sensory substitution, semantic cues, and learned
perception into one research-facing map.

Online, August 31, 2026 - The Screenless Sight Codex, created by Tristan Hash,
is now available as an open documentation project mapping visual transduction
technology from signals to perception.

The project organizes how visual information can be encoded, carried,
converted, addressed, received, and learned across different physical and
biological interfaces. Its scope includes display physics, CRT and non-CRT
display systems, signal encoding, carriers, transducers, eye optics, retinal
and cortical interfaces, sensory substitution, semantic cue systems, and
memory-adjacent frontier questions.

The codex separates established science, active research, prototype directions,
speculative architectures, and frontier ideas. Each route is framed around
concrete questions: what carries the signal, what converts it, where it enters
the user, what is perceived, what evidence exists, and what safety limits
apply.

The project is documentation-only. It is not medical advice, device-
construction guidance, or instructions for neural stimulation, implants, RF
devices, magnetic stimulation, ultrasound stimulation, or human
experimentation.

The Screenless Sight Codex is available at:
https://github.com/Tboy450/beyond-the-screen-visual-transduction
