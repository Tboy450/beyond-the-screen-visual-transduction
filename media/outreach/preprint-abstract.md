# Preprint Abstract Draft

## Working Title

The Screenless Sight Codex: Visual Transduction Technology From Displays To
Learned Perception

## Abstract

Visual technologies are often described by their final surface: screen,
projector, headset, implant, or interface. This framing can hide the deeper
engineering structure: information must be encoded, carried, converted,
addressed, received, and interpreted. The Screenless Sight Codex organizes this
structure as a modular map of visual transduction technology, moving from
conventional display systems through near-eye optics, retinal projection,
retinal and cortical prostheses, sensory substitution, semantic cue systems,
and memory-adjacent frontier concepts.

The map separates established science, active research, prototype directions,
speculative architectures, and frontier hypotheses. Each route is evaluated by
its carrier, transducer, interface point, encoding strategy, addressing method,
expected percept, evidence level, limitations, and safety boundaries. This
approach shifts the question from whether a system recreates ordinary pixels to
whether it can deliver useful, learnable perceptual cues under clear physical
and ethical constraints.

The project emphasizes that artificial or screenless perception does not need
to preserve a complete image at every layer. Event-based cues, edges, motion,
depth, symbols, semantic labels, and user-specific percept maps may be more
practical for some tasks than low-resolution image imitation. By treating
perception as an active learned component rather than a passive endpoint, the
codex provides a structured design space for assistive interfaces, prosthetic
vision concepts, and speculative visual-transduction research.
