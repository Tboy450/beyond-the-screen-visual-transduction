## Universal Copy

Name:
The Screenless Sight Codex

Full title:
The Screenless Sight Codex: Visual Transduction Technology From Signals To
Perception

Tagline:
A visual transduction map from displays to perception.

Short description:
A modular encyclopedia of visual transduction technology, from display systems
and signal carriers to prosthetic interfaces, sensory substitution, semantic
cues, and learned perception.

Long description:
The Screenless Sight Codex maps how visual information can be encoded, carried,
converted, and learned across physical and biological interfaces. It starts
with concrete display systems and moves through carriers, transducers, eye
optics, retinal and cortical interfaces, sensory substitution, semantic cueing,
and memory-adjacent frontier questions.

Established science, active research, prototype directions, speculative
architectures, and frontier ideas stay separated by evidence level. Each route
states what carries the signal, what converts it, where it enters the user,
what is perceived, and what evidence or safety limits apply.

Audience:
display technologists, assistive technology researchers, neurotechnology
readers, human-computer interaction researchers, speculative engineering
writers, and people interested in perception-centered interfaces.

Tags:
visual transduction, display technology, assistive technology, prosthetic
vision, sensory substitution, perception, neurotechnology, human-computer
interaction, speculative engineering

Safety note:
This is a documentation and research-mapping project. It is not medical advice,
device-construction guidance, or instructions for stimulation, implants, RF
devices, magnetic stimulation, ultrasound stimulation, or human experimentation.
