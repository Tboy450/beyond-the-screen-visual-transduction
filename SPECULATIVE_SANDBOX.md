# Speculative Sandbox

This page is for the bold ideas. The goal is not to prove them today. The goal
is to make them sharper, more interesting, and more testable as concepts.

Speculation format:

```text
wild idea
-> closest known science
-> missing mechanism
-> possible transducer
-> safe toy model
-> expansion path
```

## 1. Neural Scanning Interface

Wild idea: scan a controlled stimulus across a retinotopic neural or prosthetic
target over time.

Grounding analogy: raster scanning, retinal scanning displays, cortical
retinotopy, and temporal integration.

Missing mechanism: a stable way to address many perceptual locations with
enough precision and low enough risk.

Possible transducer: implanted electrode array, optogenetic target, or a future
local transducer.

Toy model: simulate a low-resolution phosphene grid on a normal monitor and
test whether people can learn to recognize shapes from scanned dots.

Expansion path: compare static dots, traced forms, moving edges, and symbolic
glyphs to see which visual language is easiest to learn.

## 2. Biological Phosphor

Wild idea: create a biological or biocompatible layer that converts a safe input
carrier into local optical or neural activity.

Grounding analogy: phosphor screens, optogenetics, fluorescent reporters,
biocompatible materials, and light-sensitive proteins.

Missing mechanism: safe, durable, addressable conversion inside or near living
tissue.

Possible transducer: engineered cells, molecular switches, or implantable
microdevices.

Toy model: draw diagrams comparing phosphor, OLED material, optogenetic
cells, and hypothetical tissue-compatible converters.

Expansion path: build a taxonomy of conversion layers by input, output, speed,
locality, reversibility, and biological compatibility.

## 3. Neural Shadow Mask

Wild idea: build the equivalent of a channel mask for neural interfaces,
preventing stimulation from spilling into the wrong perceptual channel.

Grounding analogy: display color masks, optical selectivity, and computational
methods that keep channels from bleeding into one another.

Missing mechanism: a way to restrict or shape biological activation with high
precision.

Possible transducer: geometry, timing, inhibitory control, current steering,
optical selectivity, or computation.

Toy model: model crosstalk in a grid and test algorithms that keep adjacent
"percepts" from blending.

Expansion path: explore how geometry, timing, and encoding can reduce crosstalk
in abstract maps.

## 4. Phosphene Constellation

Wild idea: accept sparse dots as dots and teach users constellations instead of
trying to make them see normal images.

Grounding analogy: people recognize star constellations, icons, Morse code,
Braille, and dashboard symbols from sparse signs.

Missing mechanism: stable dot placement and a learnable symbol vocabulary.

Possible transducer: retinal prosthesis, cortical prosthesis, tactile array, or
near-eye overlay.

Toy model: create a visual training set of sparse dot constellations for
objects, directions, hazards, and letters.

Expansion path: test whether sparse constellations can become fast symbolic
language with practice.

## 5. Scene Skeleton

Wild idea: never transmit an image at all. Transmit the skeleton of the scene:
paths, barriers, agents, hazards, and goals.

Grounding analogy: maps, architectural floor plans, video-game navigation meshes,
robot perception, and mobility aids.

Missing mechanism: reliable real-time scene understanding and a user interface
that communicates it without overload.

Possible transducer: audio, tactile, near-eye display, or future prosthetic
symbol system.

Toy model: take photos and manually annotate them into "scene skeletons,"
then compare how useful they are for navigation questions.

Expansion path: add confidence, ambiguity, and alternate interpretations to each
scene skeleton.

## 6. Synthetic Peripheral Instinct

Wild idea: create an artificial peripheral awareness field that does not show
objects clearly, but gives immediate directional awareness of motion and hazard.

Grounding analogy: peripheral vision, startle response, collision warnings, and
radar-like awareness.

Missing mechanism: a cue language that feels fast but does not cause anxiety or
false confidence.

Possible transducer: spatial audio, vibration belt, peripheral light, or sparse
phosphene cues.

Toy model: use a phone or game controller vibration pattern to represent
approaching objects in a simple simulation.

Expansion path: compare calm, urgent, directional, and graded cue vocabularies.

## 7. Dream Rehearsal Cinema

Wild idea: build a system that influences dream or memory imagery.

Grounding analogy: ordinary memory rehearsal, dream journaling, imagery
practice, context cues, and sleep-related learning research.

Missing mechanism: reliable, precise, consent-based control of dream content.

Possible transducer: ordinary sensory cues, audio prompts, images before sleep,
or journaling workflows.

Toy model: voluntary pre-sleep image and narration prompts followed by a
dream journal.

Expansion path: separate cue timing, emotional salience, repetition, and recall
accuracy.

## 8. Semantic Subtitle Track

Wild idea: instead of replacing sight, add a meaning layer to perception:
"person left," "stairs ahead," "text on sign," "cup near hand."

Grounding analogy: subtitles, screen readers, alt text, navigation assistants,
and augmented reality overlays.

Missing mechanism: low-latency recognition and a cue system that does not
interrupt the user's own perception.

Possible transducer: speech, bone conduction, spatial audio, tactile signals,
near-eye icons, or future prosthetic symbols.

Toy model: annotate a video with semantic captions and compare which cues
are actually useful.

Expansion path: create cue priorities for navigation, reading, social context,
and tool use.

## 9. Perceptual Codec

Wild idea: design a codec for the human perceptual system rather than a display.

Grounding analogy: video compression, cochlear implant coding strategies,
retinal preprocessing, and sensory substitution training.

Missing mechanism: knowing which compressed cues a person can learn quickly and
reliably.

Possible transducer: any sensory or neural output channel.

Toy model: build a table of scene inputs and compress each into five or
fewer cues, then test interpretability in ordinary visual diagrams.

Expansion path: develop a grammar for uncertainty, salience, urgency, and user
intent.

## 10. Retinotopic Address Bus

Wild idea: treat the visual pathway like an addressable bus, where location,
feature, timing, and salience are separate channels.

Grounding analogy: computer buses, raster scan timing, retinotopic maps, and
feature maps in visual cortex.

Missing mechanism: a safe way to address biological locations and features
independently.

Possible transducer: future retinal or cortical interface with closed-loop
mapping.

Toy model: create a software simulation where "visual field location,"
"feature," and "confidence" are separate encoded streams.

Expansion path: explore how location, feature, timing, and salience streams
could recombine into a learned perceptual interface.

## 11. Memory Lantern

Wild idea: a device that helps "light up" a memory without writing it.

Grounding analogy: photo albums, music-triggered recall, place memory,
flashcards, and guided imagery.

Missing mechanism: not needed if the goal is cueing rather than writing.

Possible transducer: ordinary images, sound, scent, text, location, or haptics.

Toy model: build a memory-card format where each event has image, sound,
place, emotion, and narrative cues.

Expansion path: compare which cue combinations strengthen vividness, accuracy,
and later retrieval.

## 12. Holographic Cue Field

Wild idea: use interference-like patterns to create distributed perceptual cues
instead of point-by-point stimulation.

Grounding analogy: optical holography, acoustic phased arrays, wave interference,
and population coding.

Missing mechanism: a safe, controllable biological interface that can use such
distributed patterns.

Possible transducer: optical, acoustic, or future local neural interface.

Toy model: simulate a low-resolution field where overlapping waves select
regions in a 2D map.

Expansion path: treat interference as an addressing metaphor and compare it to
array addressing and scanning.

## Sandbox Questions

Use these to expand any idea:

- What is the wild version?
- What is the tame version?
- What known technology rhymes with it?
- What is the missing transducer?
- What is the missing code?
- What would a toy model prove?
- What would it still not prove?
- Where does consent enter the design?
- What would make the idea useful even if it never becomes "full vision"?
